# Product vision

## The problem

A DM's campaign knowledge is usually distributed across documents, wikis, spreadsheets, encounter tools, rulebooks, and memory. Preparation tools often optimize for writing, while live-play tools optimize for isolated mechanics. During a session, the DM must translate between them under time pressure.

## The outcome

DM HQ should let a DM prepare information once and encounter it again in the right context:

- campaign knowledge lives in The Archive;
- session-relevant knowledge is composed in The Dashboard;
- encounter participants and references flow into The Battlefield; and
- outcomes from play return to the campaign record.

## Product principles

### Live-play attention is the scarce resource

Common actions should be fast, relevant information should remain visible, and deep navigation should not be necessary mid-session.

### One source, many useful views

An NPC is one campaign entity whether viewed in a lore page, session summary, or encounter. Components should reference shared data instead of creating copies that drift apart.

### Secrets are intentional

DM notes are private by default. Player-facing information is explicitly published, previewable, and revocable.

### Preparation can stay lightweight

A quick note can become structured later. The system should not demand a complete world model before it becomes useful.

### The campaign belongs to its group

Export, backup, and understandable data ownership are product requirements.

## Initial non-goals

The first release will not attempt to be:

- a virtual tabletop with maps, tokens, lighting, voice, or video;
- a rules compendium or replacement for purchased game content;
- an automated storyteller or autonomous DM;
- a player character builder; or
- a marketplace for campaign content.

## Candidate success measures

- Time to find an NPC or location during a session.
- Context switches outside DM HQ during play.
- Time to prepare and start a basic encounter.
- Frequency with which session outcomes are captured.
- DM confidence that private notes cannot leak to players.
